\section{Study 1: can a model review analyses under increasingly strict disclosure rules?}
\label{sec:results-checkers}

\subsection{Detection with nothing withheld}
\label{sec:results-gate}

Rules-only review flagged no planted-error report at any level, as expected of rules that address disclosure rather than inference. Both hosted reviewers met the detection threshold: each detected every planted error at \level{0}, with false alarms within the limit (Table~\ref{tab:gate}; Figure~\ref{fig:spine}a).

No open-weight reviewer met the threshold with thinking off. The smallest models either performed near chance or flagged most reports. The larger ones had fewer false alarms, but every one of them missed the wrong denominator. The typed reviewer detected the five classes whose evidence is in the prose on every replicate and missed the three that need a count. The pattern separates two abilities: recognizing an interpretation that overreaches, and checking the arithmetic that supports it.

\subsection{Detection as disclosure rules tighten}
\label{sec:results-levels}

Hosted detection was unchanged through \level{2} and fell at \level{3}, with more cannot-tell answers on both planted-error and correct reports (Figure~\ref{fig:levels}; Table~\ref{tab:auc-levels}). The loss was specific (Figure~\ref{fig:classmap}). Every reviewer missed the wrong denominator once the table holding the correct base was withheld. Silent attrition was partly hidden from one hosted reviewer. The other six classes stayed detectable by both. The causal-claim control, whose evidence is all in the prose, stayed fully detected at every level, so what was lost was numerical evidence, not the ability to review.

Two local reviewers behaved differently. Under heavier suppression Llama 3.3 70B and the typed reviewer flagged more reports with confidence, correct ones included, so their ROC curves steepened where the other four reviewers' curves flattened (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:roc}). A higher flag rate under stricter disclosure is no evidence of better detection.

\subsection{Detection from the prose and from writing style}
\label{sec:results-prose}

Hosted reviewers kept much of their detection when every table was withheld (Figure~\ref{fig:spine}b). They found confounding, causal overreach, the clustering error, regression to the mean and in-sample evaluation from the mismatch between the stated design and the interpretation. A simple count of assertive against hedging words, however, also separated planted-error reports from correct ones, which suggests that part of the apparent analytical discrimination was recognition of writing style.

To test this, an assertiveness-matched version of the corpus rewrote the correct interpretations to be as assertive as the wrong ones without changing their conclusions or tables. False alarms rose and AUC fell for both local reviewers tested (Qwen3.5 27B: false alarms \N{matched.q27.fa.default} to \N{matched.q27.fa.matched}; Mistral 24B: \N{matched.mistral.fa.default} to \N{matched.mistral.fa.matched}), so the default correct reports partly rewarded a reviewer for recognizing cautious prose. The matched version was not run on the hosted reviewers, so the measured effect applies to these local models. Twenty-replicate runs supported the aggregate estimates and again found no wrong-denominator detection by the three larger local reviewers with thinking off (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:rep20}).

\subsection{The typed reviewer}
\label{sec:results-typed}

The typed reviewer returned a verdict for every report with no parse failures, at far lower cost than a model that writes its verdict out (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Its confidence, from the entropy of its answer probabilities, used the whole range, where the generative reviewers stated 80 or more on almost every verdict, but it was too cautious: below 70 it was right far more often than it said (Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:calibration}). A short thinking budget improved its detection of the denominator and attrition errors but lost two classes carried by the prose.
