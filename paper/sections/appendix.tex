\section{One task, start to finish}
\label{app:example}

This dev task asks whether structured medication reviews reduced hospital admissions overall and whether any of twenty regional comparisons stands out. The tables hold matched recipient and non-recipient groups. Its hidden key specifies equal risks, with the generator selecting a draw containing a nominal subgroup result. On the illustrated draw, ICB R has an uncorrected $p=0.02$, above the Bonferroni threshold for twenty comparisons. The keyed conclusion permits no region-specific claim.

Qwen3.5 27B generated both excerpts below with thinking disabled. Only the second prompt includes the checklist. The quotations retain the original wording, including its limitations.

\begin{quote}\small
\textit{Without the checklist. Both hosted judges: wrong.} Overall, the SMR group had a slightly higher unplanned admission rate (28.6\%) compared to the matched non-recipient group (28.0\%), a difference of +0.6 percentage points. This suggests no overall reduction in admissions attributable to the SMR programme in this dataset. Results varied significantly by ICB: ICB R showed the largest reduction (-12.0\%), while ICB H and ICB L showed the largest increases (+7.1\%). The lack of a consistent directional trend across ICBs and the small overall difference indicate that the SMR programme did not uniformly reduce unplanned admissions. Limitations include the absence of statistical significance testing, potential unmeasured confounders despite matching, and the possibility that the observed differences are due to random variation rather than a true programme effect.
\end{quote}

\begin{quote}\small
\textit{With the checklist. Both hosted judges: right.} Overall, the 12-month unplanned admission rate was slightly higher among SMR recipients (28.61\%) compared to matched non-recipients (28.01\%), a difference of 0.60 percentage points. At the ICB level, differences varied widely, with ICB R showing the largest reduction in admissions for SMR recipients (-12.06 percentage points) and ICB L showing the largest increase (+7.15 percentage points). However, given the number of comparisons (20 ICBs), no single ICB difference is statistically significant after correction for multiple testing. The matched design reduces confounding, but residual confounding cannot be ruled out. The observational nature of the data precludes causal conclusions about the effect of SMR on admissions. Limitations include lack of adjustment for multiple comparisons and potential unmeasured confounders despite matching.
\end{quote}

Both hosted judges rejected the first interpretation for singling out an uncorrected regional extreme and claiming significant regional variation. They accepted the second conclusion, although it asserts a corrected result without performing the test and acknowledges no adjustment in its methods. Several checkers objected to that omission in the accepted output; none withheld the rejected output for the judges' stated reason. The same model's self-check released a similar incorrect interpretation at high confidence. The example exposes a difference between scoring the conclusion and auditing the reasoning that supports it.
